The seven principles this system rests on
01 · Systems over goalsYou don't try to lose 30 lbs. You become someone who moves daily. The body shape is a side effect.
02 · The 10-minute floorEvery day, no matter what — a 10-minute walk. The thing you cannot fail at.

04 · Habit stackingAnchor movement to existing cues: morning coffee, work breaks, post-dinner.

06 · Never miss twiceMissing once is life. Missing twice is the start of quitting.

Kyle's strength sessions (twice a week)

Full-body, ~20 minutes, designed around Kyle's right knee + right shoulder. Dumbbells in the apartment gym.

Mon · Push focus

Warm-up: 2 min walk in place + 10 arm circles each direction

  • Goblet squat to chair · 2 sets × 10 reps · light dumbbell, sit to chair, stand. No deep squat.
  • Incline push-up (hands on bench) · 2 sets × 8 reps · easier on shoulder than floor.
  • Romanian deadlift · 2 sets × 10 reps · light dumbbells, hinge at hips, soft knees.
  • Glute bridge · 2 sets × 12 reps · feet flat, drive through heels.
  • Dead bug · 2 sets × 8 reps each side · core, knee-friendly.

Thu · Pull focus

Warm-up: 2 min walk in place + 10 cat-cows + 10 hip circles each side

  • One-arm dumbbell row · 2 sets × 10 reps each side · braced on bench. Strong back protects shoulder.
  • Step-up (low step, no jump) · 2 sets × 8 reps each leg · controlled, no impact. Sub split squat if knee bothers.
  • Suitcase carry · 2 sets × 30 sec each side · walk holding one heavy dumbbell. Anti-rotation core.
  • Hip thrust (shoulders on bench) · 2 sets × 10 reps · glutes, knee-friendly.
  • Bird dog · 2 sets × 8 reps each side · spinal stability.

Jyselle's gentle strength sessions (twice a week)

Beginner-friendly, mostly bodyweight, ~10–15 minutes. No equipment needed at first. Goal isn't intensity — it's showing up. Once it feels easy after a few weeks, we can add a band or light weights.

Mon · Gentle full-body

Warm-up: 2 min slow walk in place + 5 shoulder rolls + 5 gentle squats (no weight)

  • Chair squat · 2 sets × 8 reps · stand up from chair, sit down. No weight needed. Builds: legs and glutes.
  • Wall push-up · 2 sets × 8 reps · stand arm's length from wall, push in and out. Builds: chest and arms gently.
  • Glute bridge · 2 sets × 10 reps · lay on back, lift hips. Wakes up your butt — most important muscle for posture.
  • Standing band row (or air row) · 2 sets × 10 reps · pull elbows back, squeeze shoulder blades. Builds: back.
  • Seated march · 2 sets × 10 reps each leg · sit tall, lift one knee at a time. Easy core work.

Thu · Gentle full-body (different angles)

Warm-up: 2 min slow walk in place + 5 cat-cows + 5 hip circles each side

  • Sit-to-stand · 2 sets × 8 reps · same as chair squat but slower on the way down. Strengthens differently.
  • Counter push-up · 2 sets × 8 reps · push-up against kitchen counter. Slightly harder than wall push-up.
  • Side steps · 2 sets × 10 each side · small steps side to side. Outer hips and balance.
  • Hip hinge (no weight) · 2 sets × 8 reps · push hips back, hands on thighs. Teaches you to bend safely from the hips.
  • Standing balance · 2 sets × 20 sec each foot · stand on one leg near a wall. Surprisingly tiring — builds ankle and core.

Jyselle's optional walk-jog method (when she feels like it)

She's open to running but new to it. The safest, most sustainable way to start: walk-jog intervals. Never starts as "running" — starts as walking with tiny jog breaks.

The first 4 weeks Walk 2 minutes, jog 30 seconds, walk 2 minutes, jog 30 seconds. Total: 15–20 minutes. Twice a week max — never on strength days. Weeks 5–8 Walk 2 minutes, jog 1 minute. Same total time. Only if the first 4 weeks felt easy. The rule If a session leaves her sore for more than a day, scale back. Soreness is not a badge — it's a signal we went too hard. The goal is to feel slightly better than when she started, not exhausted. When to skip it entirely Walk-jog is a bonus — not part of the floor. If she's tired, stressed, or sore, she just walks. The 10-minute walk is the only thing that matters for the streak.

Your data, portable

This is the export hatch. Your check-ins, streaks, and reviews are saved in your browser automatically. But systems evolve — when you eventually outgrow this dashboard, take everything with you in a single file.

How storage works on this dashboard. Your data lives in your browser's localStorage on this specific device. Kyle's phone, Jyselle's phone, your laptop — each has its own independent record. Nothing is sent to any server. Nothing is shared between devices. This is intentional: it keeps the system private, fast, and free to host on GitHub Pages.

The portability principle. Export your backup to your iCloud or Notion Memory Library every Sunday during your review. If you ever switch phones, change browsers, or migrate to a different system — strength training app, Notion database, custom build — you have a clean JSON file with every check-in and reflection you've ever logged. Data outlives any one tool.

Migration tip for future-you. The JSON file is human-readable. If you eventually want this data in Notion, the conversion is straightforward. If you want to share Kyle's progress with a coach or doctor, you can export, edit out anything personal in the reviews, and send. Your data, your decisions.

Welcome

A system for two.

This is a quiet, local-only habit dashboard. No accounts, no tracking, no cloud — just your check-ins, in your browser, on this device.

How it works:

  • The floor is a 10-minute walk every day. That's it. Everything else is bonus.
  • Check items off as you do them. The week strip lights up as you hold the floor.
  • Sundays, write a few sentences in the review. No judgment — just data.
  • Export a backup whenever you like. The Data tab will remind you every 30 days.

If you ever need to start over, the Data tab has a reset button. Your data, your decisions.
